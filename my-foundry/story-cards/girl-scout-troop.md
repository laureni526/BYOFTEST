# Story Card

## Story name

The Girl Scout troop that grew from 5 to 60

## What happened

Years later, I was leading a Girl Scout troop that started with about five girls and eventually grew to more than 60. There was no way I could personally run everything once it got that big, so the work became less about me being the troop leader and more about figuring out how other people could take ownership. We ended up with roughly 20 adults contributing in different ways. Some were comfortable leading a whole activity, while others needed something smaller and more specific to start with. I spent a lot of time matching people to work they could actually see themselves doing. The troop grew because more people were able to contribute, not because I became capable of doing 12 times as much work.

This created an opportunity not just for other women to learn how to lead in large groups, but it also created a series of role models for girls who could see their parent or other parents stepping up to contribute in small and large ways, period. It created a culture that helped everybody in this micro community understand how we can work together to solve our problems and get the job done. It did take me to be an organizer to help coordinate the logistics, but it created opportunities for other women to stretch themselves and share their talents.

## What it revealed

You can do big things when you are willing to ask for help from others. Building opportunity creates good role models and becomes an incubator for more people to raise their hands and help contribute to what you are building. It will keep growing as others get excited about where you are going.
